On the evening of April 3, Beijing time, Team M2 leader Lee convened and chaired the first six-team leads' meeting. Rather than a discussion between only two sides, the session brought together all six teams for a focused requirement-analysis meeting covering team responsibilities, target users, inter-module dependencies, and the publication of first-stage research outcomes.

## First Six-Team Leads' Meeting Marks a New Stage of Coordination

This was the first formal lead-level meeting dedicated to requirement analysis across all six teams. Initiated and chaired by Lee, it marked an important step forward as project coordination began moving from early exploratory discussion toward a more structured, system-wide alignment process.

During the meeting, participants stressed that requirement analysis is not only a preparatory task, but also a foundation for later design decisions, interface definition, and system integration. A clearer understanding of each team's position in the overall project was seen as essential for the next stage of work.

## Discussion Centred on Team Scope, Audience and Supply-Demand Questions

During the discussion, Lee emphasised that each team now needs to identify its own scope of responsibility and role within the wider system. In practical terms, this means clarifying not only what a team is expected to build, but also who its likely audience is and how its module relates to other parts of the project.

Two key questions were highlighted during the discussion.

First, each team should determine what its own module needs to obtain from other modules in the system, including data, functional support, or interface conditions.

Second, each team should define what it is expected to provide to the users or stakeholders of its own module, whether in the form of functions, information presentation, interaction logic, or service value.

Participants agreed that answering these questions clearly would help every team move beyond isolated development and towards a more connected and accountable role in the system as a whole.

## Agreement Reached on Publishing First-Stage Results and Holding a Follow-Up Meeting

At the conclusion of the meeting, the six teams agreed to publish their first-stage research findings on their respective websites. These materials will serve as visible outputs of the current requirement-analysis phase and as a shared reference for future cross-team coordination.

The teams also agreed to hold another meeting in the near future to further align requirement content across all six teams and to begin defining interfaces and related coordination matters in a more unified way.

The meeting was widely seen as practical and productive, reinforcing a common understanding of how the project should move forward. As requirement analysis continues to deepen, the broader collaboration is expected to progress with clearer structure, better-defined responsibilities, and stronger coordination across all six teams.
