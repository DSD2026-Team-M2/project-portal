At 8:00 p.m. on April 16, Lee Yihang, Wang Shu, Prof. Zhang Rui, and Dr. Yin held an online meeting to discuss the doctor-side requirements of the project. The discussion helped connect what doctors actually need to see with what the system should prioritise at the current stage, giving the team a clearer basis for defining the first-generation scope.

## Doctor-Side Requirements Became More Concrete

During the meeting, Dr. Yin explained that the doctor currently needs to see the relevant angle information when a patient performs two key movements: **knee bending** and **leg extension**. Compared with more complex or long-term display ideas, these core angle indicators are more directly tied to how doctors assess progress and follow the patient's recovery condition.

This also means that the system does not need to begin by covering every possible analytical feature. At the current stage, it is more important to clearly present the most useful motion indicators for real doctor-side judgement.

## From Motion Angles to Rehabilitation Suggestions

Beyond motion data itself, the meeting also clarified the doctor-side workflow in a more practical way. Based on the discussion, doctors will first select an appropriate rehabilitation exercise template according to the patient's angle performance in key movements, and then add extra advice where needed.

The importance of this point is that it links data display with actual intervention. The system should not only make the data visible, but also make that data naturally usable in template selection and doctor-side recommendation work.

## Agreement Reached on a First-Generation Target

After a deeper discussion of the details, the participants reached a shared agreement to treat these doctor-side requirements as one of the key targets of the first-generation project.

Although the meeting focused on a relatively narrow area, its value for the broader project was significant. It helped the team answer two practical questions more clearly: what doctors want the system to show, and what the system should provide first. That clarity also supported the larger round of later system-wide coordination.
