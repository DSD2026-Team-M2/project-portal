On the evening of April 18, the six teams held a joint meeting to align responsibilities, key data flow, and next interface tasks for the first-generation system. The shared view was clear: the most important thing at this stage is not to expand the feature list, but to make the most basic working loop actually run.

## A Working First-Generation Scope

One of the most important outcomes of the meeting was a sharper definition of what the first-generation system should include. Rather than pursuing advanced functions, the teams aligned around a minimum but operational chain: the patient side starts a session, the sensor collects data, raw input is processed into usable results, AI returns a basic recommendation, the backend stores and routes the result, and the doctor side displays it while allowing manual advice to be added.

For M2, this also made the current doctor-side focus much clearer. The meeting again confirmed that the two core motion angles remain the most important data points for the doctor view, and therefore the highest-priority indicators to make visible in the first-generation system.

## Roles Across the Six Teams Became Clearer

The meeting also made the responsibility boundary across the six teams more concrete than before. S1 is mainly focused on sensor collection and connection-related cooperation, S2 handles structured motion-data processing, V1 is responsible for lightweight template recommendation at the current stage, V2 remains the backend and data-routing hub, M1 covers the patient-side mobile workflow, and M2 continues to organise the doctor-side and admin-side web structure.

Compared with earlier, more conceptual discussion, this meeting pushed the question of who is responsible for what toward something closer to executable division of work. That clarity matters directly for interface coordination and later integration.

## From Discussion to Interface Work

The meeting also tightened the immediate rhythm for the next few days. Teams were asked to speak directly with the teams they depend on, refine interface specifications, complete system design and interface documents, and keep visible progress updates on their own websites.

In that sense, this meeting was not only an alignment session. It also marked a transition from broad discussion into a more compressed phase of concrete interface work and implementation planning. With April 30 approaching, the minimum viable first-generation loop has now become the central shared task across all six teams.
